# Hunter's Bestiary — Complete GM Guide

This guide is intentionally more detailed than the in-dashboard quick guide. It describes the complete hunt workflow, the division of responsibility between GM and players, and the anti-spam safeguards that keep investigation meaningful.

## Core principle

Hunter's Bestiary treats a hunt as an ongoing campaign project rather than a sequence of buttons. Players should be able to maintain their own records and execute most routine actions themselves. The GM remains authoritative wherever an action can reveal hidden information, advance a shared hunt, or generate repeatable mechanical progress.

The recommended default is therefore:

- **Players own their personal record-keeping and execute approved rolls.**
- **The GM controls hidden truth, shared hunt state, and whether a repeatable information-gathering opportunity currently exists.**
- **Recording an event is separate from earning progression for it.**

This separation prevents accidental "click farming" without turning the module into a GM-only interface.

## Quick setup

1. Enable Hunter's Bestiary and enter the world as a GM once.
2. Open **Configure Settings → Hunter's Bestiary**.
3. Leave **Require GM approval for repeatable player fieldwork** enabled unless your group deliberately wants unrestricted fieldwork.
4. Choose roll visibility.
5. Install or update the reference library if you use the generated Journals and Roll Tables.
6. Review the quarry catalogue and complementary providers.
7. Link or provision Actors if your system-specific companion supplies them.
8. Create a small active hunt and assign only the players taking part.

## Responsibility model

### Players can do these things independently

A player can normally:

- Open their personal Bestiary.
- Choose their active hunter.
- Read all knowledge their character has actually unlocked.
- Add and remove personal notes.
- Record sightings.
- Record trophies and samples.
- Read sources that the campaign has made available to them.
- Publish or refresh their personal Bestiary Journal.
- Inspect hunts to which they are assigned.
- Choose a permitted skill for an active hunt step.
- Execute a fieldwork or hunt attempt after the GM grants a one-use approval.
- Share an insight with other players.
- Export their own progress.

These actions are safe to expose because they either affect only the player's own record or require a separate authority check before shared progress changes.

### The GM remains authoritative over

The GM alone can:

- Create, edit, or delete quarry definitions.
- See GM secrets and hidden truth.
- Create, assign, edit, pause, advance, fail, or resolve shared hunts.
- Decide whether the fiction currently permits a new rumor search, track search, or hunt-step attempt.
- Approve or deny player fieldwork requests.
- Approve progression credit for player-recorded sightings and trophies when automatic knowledge awards are enabled.
- Directly award or remove knowledge.
- Set manual knowledge levels.
- Configure player permissions, roll visibility, and approval behavior.
- Install or update generated reference content.
- Provision or refresh managed quarry Actors.
- Change shared quarry images.
- Manage complementary providers and incomplete-package policy.
- Export or import world-wide data.

This is intentional. Those controls either expose secrets, alter shared campaign state, or can affect balance and pacing.

## Why approvals exist

Without a gate, a curious player can unintentionally turn repeated clicks into unlimited opportunities:

- asking for another rumor,
- searching for tracks again,
- rerolling the same hunt step until it succeeds,
- repeatedly recording nominally different evidence for knowledge points,
- repeatedly sharing the same insight.

The default approval model prevents that while preserving player agency.

### What an approval means

An approval means:

> "The fictional situation currently supports one attempt."

It does **not** mean:

- the action automatically succeeds,
- the player has earned a reward,
- the same action may be repeated indefinitely,
- the GM has revealed any secret information.

Rumor, track, and hunt-step approvals are single-use. When the player uses the approved action, the approval is consumed.

### Evidence is different from an attempt

Sightings and trophies are personal records, so the player may create them freely. When automatic knowledge awards are enabled, the knowledge point is not immediately granted to a non-GM player. Instead, the possible credit enters the GM approval queue.

This distinction is important:

- **Record:** "My character wrote this down."
- **Progression credit:** "This represented genuinely new, useful knowledge."

Only the second needs GM judgment.

## The GM approval queue

The GM dashboard includes **Player action approvals**.

Requests can include:

- Rumor opportunity
- Track search
- Hunt-step attempt
- Sighting credit
- Trophy/sample credit

### Approve once

Use **Approve once** when the fiction supports the requested opportunity.

For rumor, track, and hunt-step requests, approval unlocks one player action.

For sighting and trophy credit, approval immediately applies the configured automatic knowledge award and closes that request.

### Deny

Deny a request when:

- the character has no plausible access,
- nothing has changed since the last failed attempt,
- the party is not at the relevant location,
- the same evidence has already been learned,
- the request would reveal information the character has no route to obtain,
- the requested hunt stage has not actually been reached in play.

A denial should ideally be explained at the table in fictional terms.

### Revoke

An approved but unused opportunity can be revoked if the situation changes before the player acts.

## Full hunt workflow

### 1. Seed the fiction

A hunt starts with a reason to care, not a dashboard entry.

Examples:

- a patron hires the party,
- livestock disappear,
- a corpse has unusual marks,
- a road becomes unsafe,
- a witness tells a strange story,
- a research source mentions a rare creature,
- the party finds spoor while travelling,
- a faction wants a creature captured rather than killed.

At this stage, the quarry may still be uncertain.

### 2. Let players investigate naturally

Players describe what they do:

- question witnesses,
- visit taverns,
- inspect old records,
- search a kill site,
- examine a footprint,
- compare wounds,
- study a trophy,
- scout likely habitat.

When a repeatable module action becomes appropriate, the player can request it from their dashboard.

The request should follow the fiction, not replace it.

### 3. Approve one meaningful opportunity

Approve a rumor or track attempt when time, access, location, approach, and available evidence make the attempt plausible.

For a hunt step, approve the attempt when the party has actually reached the situation represented by that step.

A useful rule:

> Do not approve a second identical attempt until something meaningful changed.

Meaningful changes can include:

- a new location,
- a new witness,
- new evidence,
- a different approach,
- more time spent,
- assistance from another NPC,
- a new tool,
- accepting a new risk or cost.

### 4. Let the player execute the roll

After approval, the player chooses the permitted skill and performs the roll from their own dashboard.

The module records the result through the GM-authoritative workflow.

The GM then narrates what the result means.

### 5. Make failure change the situation

Avoid "roll until success."

A failed attempt can:

- consume time,
- trigger a complication,
- attract attention,
- spoil evidence,
- move the quarry,
- create a false lead,
- cost supplies,
- increase danger,
- close one approach but reveal another.

The module can add hunt complications, but the fiction should determine how they matter.

### 6. Award knowledge for new information

Knowledge progression should represent genuinely new understanding.

Good sources include:

- a credible new rumor,
- a distinct sign correctly interpreted,
- direct observation under risk,
- a meaningful specimen analysis,
- a newly studied source,
- a completed investigation stage,
- a resolved hunt.

Do not award separate points for multiple records that all represent the same fact.

### 7. Reach the confrontation or objective

A hunt need not end in combat.

Possible outcomes include:

- slain,
- captured,
- driven off,
- relocated,
- observed,
- negotiated with,
- escaped.

The hunt objective should reflect the campaign's actual goal.

### 8. Resolve and record consequences

When the objective is truly resolved:

1. Mark the hunt resolved.
2. Record the outcome.
3. Add relevant notes.
4. Award any final knowledge.
5. Let players record trophies, sightings, or conclusions.
6. Carry forward consequences such as faction reputation, new trade goods, ecological effects, or follow-up quests.

## Player perspective during a hunt

From the player's side, the expected loop is:

1. Learn enough to care about a quarry or assigned hunt.
2. Describe an investigative action at the table.
3. Request a rumor, track, or hunt-step opportunity when appropriate.
4. Wait for GM approval.
5. Execute the one approved attempt.
6. React to the result in the fiction.
7. Record notes, sightings, trophies, or sources.
8. Share useful knowledge with the party.
9. Repeat only when a new opportunity exists.

The player should not need the GM to enter their notes or click their routine journal controls.

## Share Insight safeguards

Share Insight remains player-friendly, but it cannot be farmed indefinitely.

A sender can award the same target player only once per quarry knowledge stage. When the sender learns enough to reach a new knowledge stage, they can share a genuinely newer insight again.

This allows cooperation without turning repeated button presses into unlimited points.

## Recommended conversation pattern

A good sequence at the table is:

**Player:** "I want to ask dockworkers whether anything unusual has been coming out of the marsh."

**GM:** Determines whether people are available, whether they know anything, and whether this is a meaningful new opportunity.

**Player:** Requests a rumor opportunity.

**GM:** Approves once.

**Player:** Makes the Investigation roll from the dashboard.

**GM:** Narrates the result and any consequence.

The same pattern works for tracks and hunt stages.

## Configure Settings recommendations

### Require GM approval for repeatable player fieldwork

Recommended: **Enabled**.

Disable only if your table explicitly wants unrestricted repeatable fieldwork and accepts that the GM will need to police rerolls socially rather than mechanically.

### Players may attempt hunt steps

Recommended: **Enabled** when approval gating is enabled.

This allows players to roll their own approved attempts while the GM still decides when an attempt exists.

### Players may add notes

Recommended: **Enabled**.

Notes are private player-owned data and do not create progression.

### Players may share knowledge

Recommended: **Enabled**.

The per-knowledge-stage deduplication prevents simple click farming.

### Automatic knowledge awards

Recommended: depends on campaign style.

When enabled:

- GM actions can still award directly.
- Player rumor/track successes can award after a one-use approved opportunity.
- player sightings/trophies queue the possible award for GM review.
- hunt-step success remains GM-authoritative through the socket workflow.

## Spoiler protection

Player interfaces intentionally do not expose:

- GM secrets,
- rumor truth labels,
- hidden quarry fields above the player's knowledge level,
- unassigned private hunts,
- other players' private progress,
- GM approval controls,
- catalogue editing,
- provider diagnostics,
- world-wide export/import controls.

Do not grant document permissions to generated GM-only sources merely because the module contains them.

## Performance and UX notes

The approval system is intentionally lightweight:

- requests are stored as compact world records,
- only pending/approved requests are rendered in the active GM queue,
- duplicate unresolved requests are coalesced,
- the queue is capped,
- progress writes remain serialized per user,
- unrelated player work remains concurrent,
- no polling loop is used,
- dashboards rerender only after meaningful state changes.

## Troubleshooting

### A player cannot request a track search

They need a known quarry or an assigned hunt target.

### A player sees "Waiting for GM approval"

There is already an unresolved request for that opportunity. The player cannot create another active copy.

### A hunt-step button says "Request one attempt"

The active step is ready in the module, but the GM has not yet confirmed that the current fictional situation allows the roll.

### A sighting was saved but gave no point

That is expected when approval gating and automatic awards are enabled. Check the GM approval queue.

### Share Insight gives no new point

The sender already shared an insight with that target at the sender's current knowledge stage. The sender must learn more before that pairing can award another point.

## Data safety

Fieldwork approvals do not change stable monster IDs, hunt IDs, provider IDs, Actor links, or player progress schema. They are stored separately in a hidden world setting. Disabling approval gating does not delete the request history.

## Hunt Rules and progressive disclosure (1.7+)

Open **GM Dashboard → Hunt Manager → Configure Hunt Rules**.

Use the global rules for campaign-wide defaults, then open a single monster override only where that creature needs different pacing. Configure:

- minimum knowledge level required before an active hunt can begin;
- which monsters participate in rumor-driven quarry selection;
- minimum level, points and successful rumors for each information field;
- per-field reveal chance;
- whether a reveal must still be approved by the GM;
- final outcome resolution policy, DC and allowed skills.

A new quarry should initially be treated as uncertain. The player may know that *something* exists without knowing its proper name, tier, defenses or weaknesses. This keeps the Bestiary an in-fiction research tool instead of a monster-stat browser.

At Resolve the Hunt, do not decide the player's intention for them. The player chooses the desired outcome. Then follow the configured policy. If GM adjudication is involved, record what actually happened even when it differs from the player's intention. An escaped quarry stays current and can be hunted again; a terminally resolved quarry ends that rumor cycle.


## 1.8 GM workflow: staged Fieldwork, deferral, and Hunt proposals

### Configure the progression once

Open **Hunt Rules** and establish the global progression defaults. The recommended default is deliberately conservative: 3 successful rumors unlock Track searching; after 5 successful Tracks, later successful Track searches can unlock Sightings; after 3 Sightings, later sightings can reveal absolute danger; and danger must be known before the hunter may propose the Hunt.

Use per-monster overrides only when ecology or campaign logic warrants a different progression. Examples: an obvious giant may reveal danger earlier; a cryptic burrower may require many Track successes; a legendary monster may require GM approval for both Sighting and danger unlocks.

The GM can also rename the five danger bands globally and override a monster's band. These labels are what players see; internal numeric tiers remain GM data.

### What the player can do

The player can perform only currently unlocked Fieldwork, record legitimate sightings after that capability is unlocked, defer the active quarry, request reactivation of a deferred quarry, and prepare a Hunt proposal once all configured prerequisites are satisfied.

The module does not let clicking substitute for fictional opportunity. Existing one-use GM approvals for repeatable checks still apply. Probabilistic unlocks are evaluated only after valid successful Fieldwork, not when the player opens the UI.

### What still requires the GM

The GM remains authoritative over:

- one-use repeatable Fieldwork opportunities when approval gating is enabled;
- any Fieldwork capability whose rule explicitly requires GM approval;
- deferred-quarry reactivation;
- every player-authored Hunt proposal;
- shared-Hunt pause/resume decisions;
- the actual final outcome of GM-adjudicated resolution.

### Deferred quarry queue

The default maximum is two deferred quarry projects and one active Hunt. Both limits are configurable. A player may freely defer the currently active quarry if space exists, but may not reactivate a deferred one without GM approval. Approval swaps it into the active slot and moves the previously active quarry to the end of the deferred queue.

If the quarry has a single-player active Hunt, deferral pauses it and approved reactivation can resume it. Multi-player Hunts are never silently paused because that would change shared state for other participants. Resolve shared-party intent at the table and adjust the Hunt explicitly as GM.

### Approving a Hunt proposal

Before approving, confirm that the proposed region, patron, stakes, template, and intended outcome match the fiction. The module rechecks the active quarry, current requirements, and active-Hunt limit at approval time.

Approval does not promise success. Treat `plannedOutcome` as the hunter's plan. At Resolve the Hunt, use the normal resolution policy and record what actually occurred.
