# Hunter's Bestiary — Detailed Player Guide

This guide explains everything a player needs to use Hunter's Bestiary without revealing GM-only information.

## The basic idea

Your Bestiary is your character's personal hunting record.

You can:

- keep notes,
- record sightings,
- record trophies and samples,
- read knowledge your character has unlocked,
- use sources the campaign makes available,
- take part in assigned hunts,
- request investigation opportunities,
- perform approved rolls yourself,
- share useful knowledge,
- publish your personal Bestiary Journal.

Some actions require a one-use GM approval because they can reveal new information or advance a shared hunt. This is not a punishment or a sign that you did something wrong. It simply keeps the hunt tied to what is actually happening in the game world.

## Open Hunter's Bestiary

You can use the normal module launchers, including:

- `Shift+B`,
- the Hunter's Bestiary Scene Control,
- the module's Token HUD button,
- Actor/Journal directory launchers,
- Configure Settings → Open Hunter's Bestiary.

## Choose your hunter

The module tries to use:

1. exactly one controlled token, or
2. the Actor assigned to your Foundry user.

The active hunter appears at the top of the dashboard.

If the wrong character is active, change your token selection or user character before making a field check.

## The Bestiary tab

The Bestiary shows only information your character currently knows.

A creature can begin as unknown or only partially understood. As your knowledge grows, more useful fields become available.

You can safely explore this page. Clicking around does not reveal hidden GM-only information.

### Personal notes

Use **Add Note** for anything your character wants to remember:

- suspected behavior,
- names of witnesses,
- places to revisit,
- theories,
- preparation lists,
- questions for the party.

Personal notes do not create knowledge points by themselves.

### Record Sighting

Use **Record Sighting** when your character actually observed the quarry or credible evidence of its presence.

The record is saved immediately.

If your campaign uses automatic knowledge awards, the possible progression credit may be sent to the GM for review. The sighting itself is still saved even before that review.

### Record Trophy

Use **Record Trophy** for a specimen, body part, trace sample, or other recovered evidence.

As with sightings, recording the trophy and receiving knowledge credit are separate things.

### Share Insight

Use **Share Insight** when your character tells another player character something useful.

Repeated clicking cannot award unlimited points. At a given knowledge stage, the same sender can award the same target only once for that quarry. Learning enough to reach a new knowledge stage can make a new share meaningful again.

## Fieldwork tab

The Fieldwork tab contains the repeatable investigation tools.

### Gather a rumor

Use this when your character has a believable way to ask around, research local talk, interview witnesses, or search informal information.

The normal flow is:

1. Describe what your character is doing at the table.
2. Click **Request a rumor opportunity**.
3. Wait for the GM to approve it.
4. The button changes to **Use approved rumor attempt**.
5. Click it once and make the Investigation check.
6. The approval is consumed by that attempt.

If you fail, you do not automatically receive another identical attempt. Change the situation or approach and discuss it with the GM.

### Search for tracks

Use this when you have a known quarry or assigned hunt and your character can physically search for signs.

The normal flow is:

1. Explain where and how you search.
2. Click **Request a track search**.
3. Wait for approval.
4. Use the approved search once.
5. Make the Survival check.
6. React to the result in the game world.

## Hunts tab

Only hunts assigned to your Foundry user are shown.

A hunt consists of ordered stages. Only the active stage can normally be attempted.

### When the active stage has a skill check

If approval gating is enabled:

1. The active step initially offers **Request one attempt**.
2. Click it after your party reaches that situation in play.
3. When the GM approves it, choose one of the allowed skills.
4. Click **Attempt**.
5. The module rolls and sends the result through the GM-authoritative workflow.
6. The one-use approval is consumed.

A failed step is not an invitation to click again until it succeeds. The GM may introduce a complication, cost, changed situation, or new route before another attempt becomes possible.

### When the active stage has no player skill check

The GM resolves or advances that stage directly.

## Library tab

The Library shows research sources included by the module.

Whether your character actually has access to a generated Journal is still controlled by normal Foundry document permissions and campaign fiction.

Use **Mark Read** after your character studies an available source.

## What you can do entirely on your own

You normally do not need GM button clicks for:

- reading unlocked Bestiary entries,
- browsing your assigned hunts,
- choosing your active hunter,
- taking personal notes,
- recording sightings,
- recording trophies/samples,
- reading a source you already have access to,
- publishing your personal Bestiary Journal,
- exporting your own progress,
- preparing a fieldwork request.

## What needs GM involvement

The GM normally decides when you receive a new opportunity to:

- gather another rumor,
- search for tracks again,
- attempt the current hunt stage,
- receive progression credit for newly recorded evidence.

This keeps the mechanics aligned with the current scene.

## What to say before requesting an action

Useful descriptions include:

- "I question the charcoal burners who work near the forest edge."
- "I compare the claw marks with the carcass we found yesterday."
- "I follow the streambed because the creature avoided the road."
- "I spend the afternoon in the guild archive."
- "I use the trophy we recovered to identify what kind of animal left these tracks."

The GM can then decide whether that creates a new opportunity.

## Why the module does not allow unlimited retries

Repeated identical checks are usually not interesting.

If nothing changed, repeated clicking can turn uncertainty into guaranteed success.

Hunter's Bestiary therefore treats an approved attempt as a specific opportunity in the fiction.

After a failure, useful next steps are:

- try a different location,
- find another witness,
- obtain better tools,
- spend more time,
- ask another character for help,
- accept a cost or risk,
- gather different evidence,
- pursue another hunt lead.

## Completing a hunt

A hunt is complete when the objective is resolved in the story, not merely when every button has been clicked.

The quarry might be:

- killed,
- captured,
- driven away,
- relocated,
- observed,
- negotiated with,
- lost or escaped.

The GM records the final outcome.

## After the hunt

Use your Bestiary to preserve what your character learned:

- write a final note,
- record a trophy,
- add the last sighting,
- read any recovered research,
- share a new insight with the party,
- publish your updated journal.

## Your data is personal

Other players do not automatically receive your complete Bestiary progress.

A party can cooperate and share discoveries, but each player's record remains independent.

## If a button is waiting for approval

That means the request is already in the GM queue. You do not need to click again.

## If an approved action disappears

One-use approvals are consumed after the corresponding attempt. Ask for another only if the fiction provides a new opportunity.

## If there is no active GM

GM-authoritative requests cannot be resolved until a GM is connected. Your personal notes and records can still be maintained.

## Rumored quarry and hidden identity (1.7+)

The first successful rumor of a new hunt cycle can introduce a new quarry chosen from the GM's campaign pool. At first, the entry may be deliberately anonymous. Seeing an entry does not mean your character knows its proper name, classification, habits, defenses or weaknesses.

Continue gathering genuinely new information. Different Bestiary fields unlock only when the campaign's configured requirements are met. Some discoveries may still require GM confirmation even after your character has gathered enough evidence.

## Hunt eligibility

A quarry may require a minimum knowledge level before an assigned hunt can begin. If the requirement is not met, continue researching, tracking, reading, observing and discussing the creature rather than trying to bypass the gate.

## Choosing the final objective

When a hunt reaches **Resolve the Hunt**, you choose what your hunter is trying to accomplish. Depending on the configured outcome, it may resolve automatically, require a field check followed by GM adjudication, or depend entirely on what happened in the scene. Your desired result is an intention, not a guarantee. The GM records the actual outcome when adjudication is required.

If the creature escapes unintentionally, it remains your current quarry so the campaign can continue the pursuit instead of silently treating the hunt as completed.


## 1.8 Fieldwork progression and quarry management

Your dashboard now tells you what kind of Fieldwork is currently available and what still needs to happen. The normal flow is:

1. Gather legitimate rumors about the current quarry.
2. Once enough successful rumors have been learned, Track searching becomes available.
3. After enough successful Track searches, later successful searches may unlock the ability to record sightings. The exact chance is campaign/monster configuration and may not be shown as secret GM information.
4. Once Sightings are available, record sightings that actually happen in play. Later sightings may reveal the quarry's absolute danger band.
5. When the configured knowledge and Fieldwork requirements are met, **Propose Hunt** becomes available.

A danger band is an absolute description of the monster, not a comparison to your character level: Low Threat, Elevated Threat, High Threat, Severe Threat, or Extreme Threat unless the GM renamed the campaign's bands.

### Deferring a quarry

If the revealed danger or current campaign situation makes a quarry inappropriate right now, use **Defer quarry**. It moves the quarry into your deferred list instead of discarding your knowledge.

To return to a deferred quarry, use **Request reactivation**. The GM must approve the switch. If another quarry is active when the request is approved, that quarry moves into the deferred queue. The campaign has a GM-configured limit for deferred quarry projects and active Hunts.

### Proposing the Hunt

When **Propose Hunt** becomes available, fill in what your hunter is trying to do: title/context, region, stakes, trail approach, intended outcome, and your plan. The proposal goes to the GM. It does not create a guaranteed result.

For example, choosing **Capture** means "I intend to capture it." The actual outcome is determined by play and GM adjudication. A failed capture can become an escape, a kill, or another logical result. If the monster escapes, it remains a valid active quarry for another Hunt.

The dashboard is designed to tell you what is available without exposing hidden monster information. Repeated button presses do not bypass prerequisites, probability rolls, one-use approvals, or GM decisions.
